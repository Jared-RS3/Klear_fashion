PROJECT OVERVIEW

The Vision

Kliya is building a fit-intelligence platform for fashion. A user captures their body once, imports a garment from a retailer, and Kliya tells them — with a stated level of confidence and a readable reason — how that garment is likely to fit them, and shows them what it looks like on.

Doing that properly means understanding two things the industry normally treats as pictures: the body and the garment. Kliya represents both as structured data, then reasons over them with its own logic — Fit-IQ — supported by Virtual Try-On visualisation and 3D garment simulation.

This proposal covers the two phases that prove that thesis technically: a discovery and technical-validation phase, and an end-to-end prototype with a physics proof of concept. The consumer MVP and beta are scoped and quoted separately once those findings are in hand.

WHAT THE PROTOTYPE MUST PROVE

  1. 01Body Intelligence — structured measurements and body profile
  2. 02Garment Intelligence — material, stretch, cut, structure and sizing
  3. 03Product import from retailer URLs and images
  4. 04Fit-IQ V1 — Kliya-owned fit-confidence logic
  5. 05Virtual Try-On visualisation
  6. 063D and physics simulation — technical proof of concept
  7. 07TrueDrape — fit-state mapping where technically supported
  8. 08Size comparison, closet, Saved Looks and wardrobe compatibility
  9. 09Creator journeys and retailer representation
→

The work is quoted as two independent phases. Phase 02 begins only after Phase 01 has been delivered, reviewed and approved in writing by Kliya — so the technical findings inform the build before the larger commitment is made.

DEVELOPMENT PROCESS

How We Will Build It

  1. 01
    DISCOVER

    Decompose the founder brief into product, technical and functional requirements, scope boundaries and the acceptance criteria the prototype will be measured against.

  2. 02
    VALIDATE

    Evaluate body-measurement, Virtual Try-On and 3D simulation providers, and establish what each can and cannot technically deliver — including output ownership and lock-in risk.

  3. 03
    ARCHITECT

    Design the application, data and integration architecture, the Garment Intelligence structure and the Kliya-owned Fit-IQ V1 logic — with providers kept modular and replaceable.

  4. 04
    BUILD

    Implement the end-to-end prototype in controlled, testable modules — body capture, product import, Garment Intelligence, Virtual Try-On, Fit-IQ and simulation.

  5. 05
    PROVE

    Run the controlled four-garment acceptance test across rigid, high-stretch, structured and fluid materials, and record honestly what is verified, what is inferred and what is not yet supported.

  6. 06
    HAND OVER

    Document the architecture, schemas, integrations and known limitations so a future Kliya CTO or engineering team can continue the work — not inherit a throwaway demo.

WHAT THE ORIGINAL PHASE 02 WAS

User photograph + garment image → third-party VTO generation → generated result.

The original proof of concept concentrated on image upload, clothing upload, VTO integration, loading states, results, retry and error handling, generation history, API tracking, testing and deployment. It was designed to answer one question: can a third-party API produce a convincing try-on image?

WHAT HAS ACTUALLY CHANGED

Body Intelligence

Structured body measurements and a persisted body profile, rather than a simple image upload.

Garment Intelligence

Structured understanding of material, stretch, cut, sizing and garment behaviour, rather than treating clothing as only an image.

Product Import

Retailer URL and image ingestion, metadata extraction, blocked-extraction handling and manual fallback.

Fit-IQ

A new Kliya-owned rules-based fit-confidence system combining body, garment and retailer sizing data.

Physics / 3D Validation

Technical integration and validation of garment and body simulation, rather than only AI image generation.

TrueDrape / Fit Mapping

User-facing fit-state representation, where supported by technically defensible simulation data.

Four-Garment Acceptance Test

The prototype must now prove considerably more than that an API returns an image — across four distinct material behaviours.

Consumer Functionality

Several capabilities previously expected later in the roadmap are now required within the Phase 02 prototype.

Creator Representation

Creator profiles, collections and the Try This Look journey now need to be represented.

Retailer Representation

The retailer value proposition and the integration-demand loop now need to be demonstrated.

Engineering Handover

The code and architecture must be suitable for continuation by a future Kliya CTO or engineering team, rather than functioning as a throwaway demo.

!
This is not an increase in the cost of delivering the original Phase 01 and Phase 02.

The technical objective and development scope have materially changed. The original Phase 02 was designed primarily to validate third-party AI Virtual Try-On through a contained proof of concept. The revised prototype now validates significantly more of the actual Kliya platform — including Body Intelligence, Garment Intelligence, product ingestion, Fit-IQ, size comparison, Virtual Try-On, physics-enhanced simulation, fit-state mapping, consumer functionality, creator journeys, retailer representation and formal four-garment technical validation. Daybreak’s development rate remains R1,100 per hour. The revised investment reflects the increase from approximately 135 hours of planned work to approximately 360 combined engineering hours.

SCOPE LIMITATIONS

Limitations and
Out of Scope

Virtual Try-On is a visualisation tool, not scientifically proven physical-fit prediction. Fit-IQ returns a stated confidence level with readable reasons — High, Moderate or Low — not a guaranteed sizing outcome, and no percentage-level physical-fit accuracy is claimed. The 3D work in Phase 02 is a technical proof of concept, not a proprietary cloth-physics engine.

TECHNICAL AND OUTPUT LIMITATIONS

  • Photograph and capture quality
  • Body position and lighting
  • Garment-image quality
  • Transparent and layered fabrics
  • Oversized and unstructured garments
  • Complex patterns, logos and text
  • Fabric movement and drape
  • Missing side or rear product images
  • Incomplete or absent retailer size charts
  • Blocked or unavailable product-page extraction
  • Inferred rather than verified garment attributes
  • Tension, strain and pressure data only where the selected provider exposes it — never fabricated
  • Provider latency, reliability and rate limits

NOT INCLUDED IN THIS PROPOSAL

  • Proprietary Kliya neural cloth-physics engine
  • Scientifically validated physical-fit claims
  • Guaranteed sizing accuracy
  • Production-scale retailer integrations
  • Marketplace checkout
  • Creator payouts
  • International tax infrastructure
  • Full native iOS and Android production applications
  • Production-scale social network
  • Full moderation infrastructure
  • Interior / home visual-commerce module
  • Ongoing CTO responsibility
  • Third-party APIs
  • Third-party software licences
  • Hosting, cloud and vendor charges
  • Legal services
  • Brand development and marketing
