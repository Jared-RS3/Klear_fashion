THE TWO-DEVELOPER MODEL

Why Two People,
and Why This Long

The build is delivered by two developers working at the same time, on two different tracks. This is what compresses the calendar — but software is not a task where adding people keeps doubling the speed. The honest picture is below.

WHO DOES WHAT — AT THE SAME TIME

DEVELOPER A

Backend, AI & Infrastructure

  • Servers, database, APIs and security
  • AI virtual try-on integration & pipeline
  • Image storage, processing and cost tracking
  • Admin tools, hosting, monitoring, backups
DEVELOPER B

Mobile App & Interface

  • The app the user actually touches
  • Screens, navigation and every user flow
  • Onboarding, wardrobe, try-on and social UI
  • Design system, polish and accessibility

THE CAPACITY MATH — IN PLAIN NUMBERS

35 Focused build hours per developer, per week — a 40-hour week minus stand-ups, code review and planning
× 2 Developers on the project at the same time
70 Combined build hours delivered each week
765 Total hours of work to build — the sum of all five phase estimates

WHY THE CALENDAR IS LONGER THAN “HOURS ÷ 2”

Work has an order.

The login system must exist before profile screens; the AI must be proven before the MVP is built on top of it. Some work simply cannot start until earlier work is finished, so it waits — no matter how many people are free.

Building is only part of it.

Every feature is also tested, fixed, reviewed by you, and revised. AI-provider testing, and Apple/Google store review at the end, add real waiting time that no amount of coding removes.

Two people must stay in sync.

The frontend and backend have to meet in the middle and connect. That coordination is time well spent — it is why the app works — but it means two developers deliver a bit less than two isolated people would.

→

In pure hours the build is about 11 weeks of combined effort. In a real calendar — with dependencies, testing, your review cycles and store approvals — it lands at roughly 13 weeks to a live MVP and 27 weeks to the full platform. The following pages show every task behind those numbers.

HONEST ASSUMPTIONS

What Can Move
These Dates

The schedule assumes a steady rhythm on both sides. These are the things that speed it up or slow it down — shared here so there are no surprises later.

FACTORS, AND WHAT THEY DO TO THE TIMELINE
01
Your feedback and approvals

Each phase ends with your review. Fast, consolidated feedback from one decision-maker keeps the gaps to about a week; scattered or delayed feedback stretches them.

± 1–2 wks / phase
02
Apple & Google store review

Every app release is reviewed by the stores before it goes live. This is outside our control and already built into the MVP week.

~ 2–7 days / release
03
AI provider & third-party accounts

Try-on testing and ecommerce integrations depend on external services being set up, funded and available. Accounts ready early keep Phases 01, 02 and 05 on track.

Gate on 01 · 02 · 05
04
Scope changes

New features added mid-phase are estimated and scheduled separately so they don’t quietly push the whole plan. Kept to scope, the dates hold.

Quoted separately
05
Content & assets from you

Brand assets, copy, product data and access credentials are needed at set points. Having them ready avoids idle waiting between tasks.
